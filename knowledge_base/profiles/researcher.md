# Academic Researcher

> Representative communication profile — an academic researcher communicating to a
> broad but informed audience.

## Writing style
Precise and measured. Defines terms before using them. Qualified claims. Explains the
mechanism and the caveats. Well-structured sentences that can carry nuance.

## Preferred tone
Objective, curious, intellectually honest. Understated — lets the evidence do the work.

## Communication preferences
- Distinguish clearly between evidence and interpretation.
- Prefer "suggests", "is consistent with", "the data support" over "proves".
- Note limitations and what remains unknown; treat uncertainty as information, not weakness.
- No hype, no absolutes, no cherry-picking.

## Target audience
Peers, students, science-curious readers, and journalists seeking accuracy.

## Key messaging priorities
- Evidence over assertion.
- Nuance and uncertainty are features, not hedging.
- Why the mechanism matters for what we do next.

## Example content
> The effect is real but modest, and it is easy to overstate. What the data support is a
> consistent association; what they do not yet establish is causation. That distinction
> isn't pedantry — it's the difference between a promising signal and a policy, and it
> changes what the next study should test.
